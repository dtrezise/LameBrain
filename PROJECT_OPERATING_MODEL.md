# Lame Brain Ideas — Company operating model

**Version:** 1.0
**Effective:** 2026-10-07
**Stage:** founding pilot

## Purpose

The operating model keeps commercial speed, research integrity, customer trust, and financial discipline under named human accountability. AI and automation may assist; they never hold accountability or approve a customer deliverable.

## Decision rights

| Decision | Accountable role | Required review |
|---|---|---|
| Company strategy, capital release, brand, scope | Founder / General Manager | Finance for capital; counsel for regulated or high-risk scope |
| Offer definition, pricing, sales promise | Commercial Lead | Delivery and Finance |
| Customer intake, scope, quality, referral | Delivery Lead | Research & Integrity for material claims |
| Evidence standards, sources, corrections | Research & Integrity Lead | Domain specialist for high-risk claims |
| Privacy, approved tools, retention, incidents | Trust & Operations Lead | Counsel and security specialist as required |
| Partnerships and community programs | Partnerships Lead | Delivery capacity and consent review |
| Podcast, video, newsletter, public stories | Editorial Lead | Consent and factual review |
| Model, budget, cash, invoices, KPIs | Finance & Administration Lead | Founder approval for capital release |

One person may hold several roles during the pilot, but each decision must still be recorded under the applicable role. “AI,” “agent,” or “the system” is never an owner.

## Minimum launch team

### Founder / General Manager

- Owns the pilot thesis, capital ceiling, hiring, professional appointments, brand clearance, and stop/go decisions.
- Approves exceptions and signs contracts.

### Delivery Lead / Brain Stormer

- Runs discovery, defines scope, produces the engagement artifact, records time and evidence, and owns follow-through.
- Stops work when specialist advice or unsafe scope is required.

### Research & Integrity Lead

- Maintains the evidence register, verifies material claims, resolves citation gaps, logs corrections, and grades confidence.
- Runs a pre-publication review for every public research artifact.

### Partnerships and Community Lead

- Recruits pilot hosts, manages sponsored cohorts, attributes leads, and documents partner outcomes.

### Editorial Lead

- Runs the public-learning calendar, source notes, transcripts, separate media consent, and corrections.

### Trust, Finance, and Administration

- Maintains approved tools, contracts, privacy and retention records, insurance, bookkeeping, model actuals, tax handoff, and cash gates.

At pilot scale these duties may be combined between the founder, a senior operator, and fractional specialists. Separation of duties is required for outgoing payments, refunds, public corrections, and any material capital release.

## External specialists

- Business and privacy counsel before customer intake
- Trademark counsel for name and identity clearance
- CPA/bookkeeper for entity, tax, payroll, and monthly close
- Insurance broker for general liability, professional liability, cyber, equipment, workers compensation, and event needs
- Patent attorney or agent for patentability or filing questions
- Licensed professionals for legal, medical, investment, engineering, or safety matters
- Accessibility specialist for customer and public artifacts
- Securities counsel plus a registered intermediary before any future securities-related activity

## Service boundary

The launch company provides facilitation, research synthesis, planning, prototyping briefs, operating models, and launch-readiness work. It does not provide:

- legal, tax, medical, investment, engineering certification, or patentability opinions
- custody of customer funds or investments
- investor matching, securities recommendations, or transaction-based compensation
- guarantees of funding, IP protection, commercial success, media reach, or investor interest

## Stage-gate governance

### Gate 0 — Ready to accept a customer

Entity, approved agreements, privacy/AI-use schedule, insurance, payment/refund process, data locations, incident plan, evidence template, and accessibility path are operational.

### Gate 1 — Paid demand by Month 3

- 3 active host partners
- 12 paid cases across at least 2 offers
- source-attributed leads and conversion
- no unresolved material trust incident

### Gate 2 — Repeatable delivery by Month 6

- time, rework, direct cost, refund, satisfaction, and outcome data complete for at least 80% of cases
- trailing contribution margin at or above 50%, or a documented redesign
- at least 30% of completed customers produce a qualified referral, follow-on need, or explicit continuation request

### Gate 3 — Scale decision by Month 12

- one segment and two offers show repeatable acquisition and delivery
- capacity plan keeps utilization below 85%
- one-year cash and downside plan are current
- a permanent location, membership, or material hire has a separate evidence-backed investment memo

Failure to meet a gate triggers a recorded choice to narrow, redesign, pause, refer, or stop. It does not automatically trigger more marketing spend.

## Weekly operating cadence

- **Monday — pipeline and capacity:** leads, attribution, booking, paid conversion, backlog, and delivery load.
- **Wednesday — case and evidence review:** scope risks, source quality, rework, customer decisions, and referrals.
- **Friday — cash and learning:** invoices, collections, direct cost, runway, incidents, corrections, and what changed.
- **Monthly — operating review:** actual-versus-model, cohort outcomes, editorial performance, partner health, and gate status.

## Canonical records

- Engagement scope and consent record
- Case evidence register
- Time and cost log
- Customer outcome and follow-up record
- Funnel and channel register
- Model actuals and monthly close
- Public-source and corrections register
- Tool approval and data-retention register
- Incident log

## Quality rule

Every material deliverable must answer four questions:

1. What decision is this for?
2. What is fact, estimate, assumption, target, or unknown?
3. What evidence would change the recommendation?
4. Who is accountable for the final judgment?
