# Lame Brain Ideas - Market Research and Feasibility Evidence

- **Research date:** 2026-07-17
- **Geography:** United States, with a future pilot market to be selected
- **Interpretation:** Current market signals plus a bottom-up pilot thesis; not a commissioned primary-research study

## Executive finding

There is a large and continually replenished population of aspiring founders, creators, nonemployer businesses, and small employers. The market does **not** prove that these customers want a retail store or will pay for broad "idea help." Lame Brain's feasible wedge is a bounded, outcome-based service that produces trusted deliverables faster and more accessibly than an agency, with more structure and tangible execution than free mentoring or self-service AI.

The appropriate conclusion is a **conditional go for a lean paid pilot** and a **no-go for immediate flagship, proprietary crowdfunding platform, investor marketplace, or franchising**.

## Demand signals

| Signal | Current evidence | Implication | Limitation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| U.S. small-business base | 36,207,130 small businesses; 29.8 million have no employees and 6.4 million have employees | Large addressable population that routinely needs planning, research, branding, and financing help | Existing firms are not automatically prospects |
| New-business intent | 531,423 seasonally adjusted U.S. business applications in June 2026 | The idea-to-business funnel is replenished every month | An EIN application is not a purchase signal for Lame Brain |
| Near-term formation | 29,741 June 2026 applications were projected to form payroll businesses within four quarters | A meaningful subset advances beyond casual ideation | The majority of applications will not become employer firms |
| Establishment churn | About 1.3 million establishments opened for the first time in 2023; about 1.2 million closed | Founders face both launch demand and high failure risk | Historical national statistic, not metro demand |
| AI adoption | 7.6% of businesses used AI between September 2024 and August 2025; 8.2% for firms with fewer than five employees | AI interest exists but expert-guided adoption remains far from universal | Usage does not show willingness to pay Lame Brain |
| Consulting labor benchmark | Management analysts: $101,190 median annual wage; market-research analysts: $76,950 | Skilled delivery requires professional compensation and supports premium outcome pricing | Employee wages are not equivalent to consulting prices |
| Retail availability | Q1 2026 U.S. retail availability was 4.9%; average asking rent was $24.59/sq. ft. | Attractive small-format space may be scarce; model should not use bargain rents | National averages exclude local NNN/CAM and build-out variation |

## Market size discipline

The concept is too early for a defensible top-down TAM. A credible market case should use three layers:

1. **Demand universe:** aspiring founders, active creators, nonemployer businesses, and small employers.
2. **Serviceable annual event pool:** people in a chosen metro who are actively validating, launching, or funding an idea in the next 12 months.
3. **Obtainable pilot demand:** the transactions and memberships one store can acquire and fulfill at acceptable contribution margin.

For the pilot, capacity is the binding constraint. Five delivery FTE at 1,450 available client hours each yield 7,250 annual client-service hours. The base model ramps paid utilization from 38% in Year 1 to 82% in Year 5. That is more decision-useful than multiplying the national small-business count by an arbitrary share.

The recommended pre-lease research must quantify:

- unaided and aided awareness of the problem
- interest in the promise, not just the name
- willingness to pay at $99, $649, $3,750, and $12,500 working price points
- conversion from free diagnostic to paid work
- acquisition cost by channel
- completion time, rework, and customer satisfaction
- demand by idea type and the expertise required to serve it safely

## Customer segments

| Segment | Job to be done | Best initial offer | Why it may pay |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curious inventor / maker | Turn a rough concept into a coherent problem, user, solution, and next experiment | Quick Diagnostic or Idea Sprint | Clarity, momentum, and tangible artifact |
| First-time founder | Validate demand, business model, costs, and launch path | Idea Sprint or Venture Build | Avoids expensive false starts and consultant fragmentation |
| Creator / author / filmmaker / game maker | Package a creative project and test audience/funding readiness | Venture Build | Strong fit with rewards crowdfunding and community building |
| Existing small-business owner | Develop a new product, service, or location | Venture Build or Launch Studio | Has operating context and higher ability to pay |
| Corporate/community innovation team | Facilitate structured ideation and prototype a concept | Private Workshop / Launch Studio | Budget, group use, and repeatability |
| Student / youth innovator | Learn and package an idea | Sponsored cohort | Mission and pipeline value, but limited direct ability to pay |

## Competitive structure

| Alternative | Customer value | Lame Brain disadvantage | Lame Brain response |
|---|---|---|---|
| SCORE | Free, confidential mentoring and business education | Free is difficult to beat for general advice | Sell production, research synthesis, facilitation, prototyping, and accountable completion |
| SBA Small Business Development Centers | Individual advising and technical assistance for pre-venture and small businesses | Trusted public infrastructure and broad expertise | Partner/referral strategy; avoid duplicating regulated or subsidized services |
| Self-service generative AI | Immediate and inexpensive brainstorming and drafting | Customers can generate generic plans at near-zero marginal cost | Human facilitation, evidence standards, confidential workflow, synthesis, and implementation artifacts |
| Freelancers and agencies | Specialized design, research, engineering, and branding | Deeper specialist craft in a narrow lane | Curate the journey and partner network; do not pretend every Brain Stormer is every specialist |
| Accelerators/incubators | Cohort, network, education, capital access | Often free or funded through equity; high signaling value | Serve earlier and broader demand; provide a paid on-ramp and readiness package |
| Maker spaces | Equipment, classes, and community | Better physical prototyping infrastructure | Partner rather than overbuild a low-utilization lab |
| Kickstarter | Large rewards-crowdfunding brand; 5% platform fee plus roughly 3%-5% payment processing | Existing backer network and mature infrastructure | Offer campaign-readiness and production services before considering an owned marketplace |
| Indiegogo | Established campaign infrastructure; 5% platform fee plus processing | Platform, payments, and campaign operations already exist | Partner or refer; charge for preparation and execution support |
| Regulation Crowdfunding portals | Registered securities infrastructure and investor processes | High compliance burden and concentrated incumbent market | Use a qualified intermediary; do not operate an unregistered portal |

## Crowdfunding and investor-market evidence

Rewards crowdfunding validates a creator market, but platform revenue is competitive and operationally difficult. Kickstarter and Indiegogo both advertise 5% platform fees, leaving limited room to undercut without scale or a differentiated audience.

Securities crowdfunding is growing but regulated. SEC data through December 31, 2025 reports 9,461 Regulation Crowdfunding offerings, 4,303 offerings reporting proceeds, and $1.546 billion in total reported capital raised since May 2016. The average offering reporting proceeds raised about $359,000. These figures support the existence of a market but also show that an intermediary business needs compliance, issuer quality, investor acquisition, and scale.

Regulation Crowdfunding offerings must be conducted through a single SEC-registered broker-dealer or funding portal that is a FINRA member. Eligible issuers may raise up to $5 million in a 12-month period, subject to disclosures and other conditions. Lame Brain should initially partner with an existing intermediary and keep its own service fees separate from securities transaction compensation unless counsel approves a structure.

## Retail and location thesis

The storefront is a trust and acquisition mechanism, not the primary moat. A pilot should target approximately 2,800-3,500 square feet in a service-oriented, walkable suburban or mixed-use district with evening/weekend access, visible parking/transit, and proximity to universities, maker communities, professional services, and small-business networks.

National Q1 2026 asking rent averaged $24.59 per square foot and availability was 4.9%. The financial model uses a deliberately higher $30 base rent plus $9 NNN/CAM assumption for a visible, customer-ready location. The actual pilot decision requires three broker-backed lease proposals and contractor estimates.

Candidate cities from the source—Austin, Nashville, Raleigh, Pittsburgh, and Boulder—remain a hypothesis list. A final selection should score:

- high-propensity business applications per adult
- self-employment and nonemployer density
- household income and founder/customer affordability
- university, maker, creative, and startup ecosystem density
- retail occupancy cost and tenant-improvement economics
- professional talent availability and wage cost
- airport and regional access
- partnership density: SBDC, SCORE, libraries, universities, incubators, maker spaces, and investors
- brand fit and accessibility

## Pricing thesis

The source's $49/$199/$999/$5,000+ ladder is useful for accessibility but cannot support a professional retail labor model at scale. The feasibility model tests the following working prices:

| Offer | Working price | Delivery hours | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Quick Diagnostic | $99 | 1 | Define the idea, risk, and next step |
| Idea Sprint | $649 | 4 | Structured concept, initial validation, and test plan |
| Venture Build | $3,750 | 20 | Market, model, prototype brief, economics, and execution roadmap |
| Launch Studio | $12,500 | 60 | Integrated package and launch/funding readiness |
| Founder Membership | $99/month | Group/digital support | Retention, accountability, office hours, and community |

These are model inputs, not validated prices. The pre-lease pilot must test price integrity, scope, and gross margin.

## Feasibility scorecard

| Dimension | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Problem relevance | Strong | Large numbers of people and small firms confront fragmented idea-to-launch work |
| Category differentiation | Promising | Physical, human-guided idea retail is memorable and legible |
| Proven demand | Weak | No primary customer evidence or paid pilot yet |
| Launch regulatory burden | Manageable | Phase 1 can be a consulting/studio business with clear boundaries |
| Later regulatory burden | High | Securities, investor matching, IP advice, equity, and franchising require specialist structures |
| Unit economics | Conditional | Premium work and memberships can cover costs; low-price sessions alone cannot |
| Capital efficiency | Weak as flagship-first; strong as pop-up-first | A lean service pilot can test the thesis before heavy build-out |
| Scalability | Conditional | Depends on workflow standardization, quality assurance, training, and customer-data governance |
| Defensibility | Moderate | Brand, operating data, workflow system, outcomes, and network can compound; generic AI cannot |
