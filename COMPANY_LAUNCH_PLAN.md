# Lame Brain Ideas — Company launch plan

**Planning date:** 2026-10-07
**Launch posture:** lean paid pilot
**Planning horizon:** 24 months

## Executive decision

Form and launch Lame Brain Ideas as a portable, partner-hosted and digital idea-development studio. Authorize no more than **$125,000 for the first 90 days**, released in three evidence-based tranches. Plan for approximately **$226,000 of total external funding** over 24 months in the management case. Do not sign a flagship lease or enter regulated capital activity.

The immediate job is not to prove that “ideas” are a large market. It is to prove that a specific customer will pay for a specific outcome, that the outcome can be delivered responsibly at acceptable contribution, and that trusted channels can repeatedly reach that customer.

## Company thesis

People can generate text cheaply. They still struggle to frame the right decision, find and assess evidence, integrate specialist judgment, manage uncertainty, and finish a useful artifact. Lame Brain Ideas can earn a place between self-service AI, free mentoring, fragmented freelancers, and expensive agencies by making that work legible, bounded, and accountable.

### Positioning

**For people and organizations with a rough idea, Lame Brain Ideas is a human-guided decision studio that turns uncertainty into evidence, artifacts, and responsible next steps. Unlike a blank AI chat, generic coaching, or a full agency, it combines facilitation, research standards, practical production, and explicit stop/go judgment.**

### Initial segments

1. **First-time and non-technical builders** with enough urgency and ability to pay for a bounded diagnostic or sprint.
2. **Existing small-business owners** testing a new product, service, location, or channel.
3. **Community, education, and economic-development hosts** buying structured clinics or sponsored cohorts.
4. **Creators and makers** needing an evidence and launch-readiness package, excluding regulated funding advice.

The pilot should not advertise “any idea for anyone.” Intake should favor decisions that can be responsibly advanced with business research, facilitation, prototyping briefs, and operating planning.

## Go-to-market system

### Channel 1 — Trusted hosts

Recruit libraries, maker spaces, universities, SBDCs, community organizations, chambers, coworking spaces, and professional firms. Offer one clearly scoped clinic or workshop, a host briefing, participant consent, and an outcomes report. Measure every lead and paid engagement by host.

### Channel 2 — Founder referrals

Ask after a documented customer outcome, not at checkout. Track introductions, follow-on work, and unresolved objections. Never pay transaction-based compensation for capital introductions.

### Channel 3 — Public learning

Produce an eight-episode founding season across audio and video:

- two Idea Clinics
- two Builder Stories
- two Evidence Reviews
- two Field Notes from the pilot

Each episode has one qualified action: submit a decision, host a clinic, propose an expert referral, or inspect the evidence. Paid service never requires media participation.

### Channel 4 — Professional referrals

Build a small, vetted referral bench for legal, tax, IP, engineering, design, accessibility, and finance questions. Publish clear boundaries and avoid implying endorsement beyond a documented introduction.

## The first 90 days

### Days 0–30 — Form, protect, recruit

- Form the entity, open banking and bookkeeping, bind insurance, approve customer and partner agreements.
- Complete counsel-led name clearance and moderate name testing.
- Provision the public email and payment/refund path.
- Approve privacy, AI-use, data-retention, consent, incident, and referral procedures.
- Sign 3 pilot hosts and recruit at least 30 qualified discovery participants.
- Train delivery against one case template and run two internal simulations.
- Publish the site, public brief, evidence register, and the first two media pieces.

**Release condition for Tranche 2:** controls approved, three credible hosts, functioning intake/payment, and no unresolved brand or insurance blocker.

### Days 31–60 — Sell and deliver

- Convert first paid cases across at least two offers.
- Log acquisition source, scope, price, time, direct cost, rework, refund, outcome, and referral intent.
- Hold a twice-weekly case review and weekly cash review.
- Publish two more media pieces with separate consent and traceable sources.
- Review price and scope only after at least six completed paid cases.

**Release condition for Tranche 3:** six paid cases, complete delivery economics, acceptable customer outcomes, and no material trust incident.

### Days 61–90 — Reconcile and decide

- Reach the target of 12 paid cases or document why the channel/offer failed.
- Complete eight public episodes or explicitly revise the editorial thesis.
- Replace model inputs with actual conversion, price, mix, delivery time, cost, and refund data.
- Select the most promising segment and two offers.
- Decide: repeat, narrow, redesign, pause, or stop.

## Months 4–12 — Repeatability

- Retain only offers with evidence of demand, outcome, and contribution.
- Standardize research and artifact templates without making them generic.
- Keep monthly delivery utilization below 85% and protect review capacity.
- Add contractor or part-time operational support only when backlog, quality, and cash gates are met.
- Expand to 6–10 active host relationships in one region before national outreach.
- Publish monthly evidence notes and quarterly corrections/model updates.
- Do not add a lease. Test physical experience through hosted residencies and pop-ups.

## Months 13–24 — Selective expansion

- Test a $29/month continuity membership with one paid cohort only if customers request ongoing support.
- Add regional partner cohorts only after one repeatable local playbook.
- Test media sponsorship after audience quality, editorial independence, disclosure, and advertiser fit are documented.
- Prepare a separate location memo only if partner-hosted capacity or customer experience creates a quantified constraint.
- Any rewards-crowdfunding preparation service remains advisory and execution-oriented; any marketplace or securities concept requires a new legal and investment case.

## Principal risks and responses

| Risk | Early warning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity does not convert | High interest, low paid conversion | Narrow segment and outcome; stop broad awareness spend |
| Scope becomes unbounded | Rework and delivery hours exceed plan | Tighten intake, exclusions, change control, and referrals |
| Generic AI erodes value | Customers compare only on document output | Lead with judgment, evidence, facilitation, and follow-through |
| Brand repels intended users | Negative or ableist interpretation in testing | Rename before material spend; do not defend the hypothesis emotionally |
| Confidentiality or IP incident | Unapproved tool or unclear consent | Stop work, contain, notify per plan, remediate, and document |
| Media conflicts with service | Customers feel pressured to publish | Separate editorial staff, consent, and commercial terms |
| Growth outruns review capacity | Utilization above 85%, missed QA | Waitlist, raise price, add reviewed capacity, or reduce scope |
| Founder concentration | Decisions and relationships depend on one person | Document playbooks, delegate roles, and maintain succession coverage |

## Definition of company-launch readiness

The company is ready to accept its first external paid customer only when every Gate 0 item in `LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md` has evidence, an owner, and a date. A polished site alone is not launch readiness.
