# Trust and data policy

**Status:** pilot operating standard pending counsel approval
**Effective:** 2026-10-07

## Principles

1. Collect less.
2. Use information only for the agreed purpose.
3. Keep a named human accountable.
4. Separate paid service from publicity and marketing consent.
5. Distinguish fact, estimate, assumption, target, and unknown.
6. Refer high-risk judgments to qualified specialists.
7. Make correction and deletion possible.

## Data classes

| Class | Examples | Default handling |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Published sources, public company information | May be used with attribution and rights review |
| Ordinary customer | Contact, project summary, scheduling, invoices | Approved business systems; role-based access |
| Confidential project | Nonpublic plans, research, prototypes, contracts | Engagement-specific approval, minimum access, no public AI tools |
| Restricted | Credentials, account numbers, health data, biometrics, export-controlled or highly sensitive IP | Do not collect unless a specialist-approved system and purpose exist |

## Intake

The public site asks only for contact details, starting point, and a one-sentence goal. It uses a local `mailto:` action and does not store the entry on the site. Prospects are warned not to send confidential or restricted information before handling terms are agreed.

## Engagement data schedule

Each engagement must document:

- data owner and customer authority
- types and sensitivity
- approved systems and model providers
- access roster
- purpose and permitted outputs
- storage region/location if material
- retention date and deletion method
- customer export/return method
- incident contacts
- media and portfolio status

## AI use

- AI tools require an approved-tool record covering terms, training/data use, retention, access, security, and intended task.
- Do not place confidential or restricted customer content into a tool not explicitly approved for that engagement.
- Keep source material and human review proportionate to consequence.
- Document material human creative and analytical contribution when ownership matters.
- Prompts alone are not treated as proof of copyrightable authorship.
- AI cannot approve a customer recommendation, correction, refund, publication, or high-risk decision.

## Research integrity

- Prefer primary sources for material claims.
- Record publication and access dates.
- Link a source to the exact claim it supports.
- State limitations, contradictory evidence, and staleness.
- Label customer-provided and management-assumption data.
- Avoid false precision and top-down market-share math without a causal acquisition/capacity model.

## Media and marketing

Media consent is separate from service consent. A media release must specify the project elements, name/likeness, channels, editing review if any, revocation deadline, and compensation/sponsor status. No customer is disadvantaged for declining.

Marketing uses only consented contacts. An email program must provide source, purpose, unsubscribe, suppression, retention, and accessibility controls before activation.

## Retention and deletion

The default target is:

- prospect inquiries not converted: 90 days
- engagement working files: term stated in the SOW, then delete or archive only for a documented legal/business need
- contracts, invoices, and tax records: statutory/business retention set by counsel and CPA
- consent and correction records: for the life of the published use plus a reasonable defense period
- de-identified operating metrics: retained only when re-identification risk is acceptably low

Actual periods must be approved before launch and take precedence over these planning defaults.

## Incidents

Anyone who suspects unauthorized access, disclosure, incorrect publication, harmful output, lost device, or unapproved tool use must stop the affected work and notify the Trust owner. The response record includes discovery time, systems/data, containment, evidence preservation, legal notification decision, customer communication, remediation, and closure.

## Rights and requests

Provide a clear route for access, correction, deletion, objection, accommodation, and complaint. Identity should be verified proportionately without collecting unnecessary new sensitive data.

## Accessibility

Public and customer-facing work targets keyboard operation, semantic structure, visible focus, sufficient contrast, zoom/reflow, reduced-motion support, captions/transcripts, meaningful link text, accessible tables, and alternative formats. Critical barriers block publication or require a disclosed accommodation path.

## Change control

The Founder, Trust owner, and counsel approve material changes to data categories, tools, purposes, retention, media use, or regulated scope. A changed website or model does not silently change customer agreements.
