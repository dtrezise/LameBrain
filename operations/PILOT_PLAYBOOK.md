# Founding pilot playbook

## Objective

Prove or disprove the smallest viable company: a human-guided, evidence-first idea studio that customers and host partners will pay for, that produces useful decisions, and that can deliver at acceptable contribution without compromising trust.

## Case lifecycle

### 1. Qualify

Ask only:

- What decision are you trying to make?
- Who will use the outcome?
- What has already been tried?
- What deadline or event creates urgency?
- What information is sensitive?
- What would a useful next step look like?

Do not request trade secrets, health information, financial credentials, unfiled invention details, or regulated records during initial qualification.

### 2. Accept, refer, or decline

Accept when the work can be advanced through facilitation, public/approved research, planning, an operating model, and a bounded artifact. Refer or decline when the central judgment requires legal, tax, patent, investment, medical, safety, engineering certification, or another licensed specialty.

Document the decision and the reason. A referral is a successful outcome when it prevents false confidence.

### 3. Scope

Every statement of work specifies:

- decision and intended user
- deliverables and exclusions
- price, payment, schedule, and revision allowance
- customer responsibilities
- approved information, tools, and collaborators
- ownership/licensing and third-party materials
- retention/deletion and media consent status
- change-control and stop conditions

### 4. Discover

Create a one-page decision frame:

| Field | Required entry |
|---|---|
| Decision | One sentence |
| Deadline | Date and reason |
| People affected | Users, buyers, operators, approvers |
| Success | Observable change |
| Constraints | Money, time, law, safety, capability |
| Known | Supported facts |
| Believed | Assumptions and source |
| Unknown | Evidence needed |
| Stop conditions | What would make the idea irresponsible or uneconomic |

### 5. Research and build

- Prefer primary and authoritative sources.
- Record title, publisher, URL, publication date, access date, claim supported, limitations, and owner.
- Mark customer statements as customer-provided rather than independently verified.
- Label calculations and management assumptions.
- Use AI only through approved tools and within the engagement data schedule.
- Maintain a human decision log when AI materially shapes a recommendation.

### 6. Review

Before delivery, a reviewer other than the primary author checks:

- scope and exclusions
- claim/source fit and freshness
- calculations, units, and time periods
- uncertainty and counter-evidence
- copyright, trademark, and customer-material handling
- accessibility and usable file formats
- clarity of recommendation and next test

High-risk claims require a qualified domain reviewer or removal.

### 7. Deliver and decide

The closing conversation answers:

1. What did we learn?
2. What changed from the initial belief?
3. What should happen now: proceed, revise, pause, refer, or stop?
4. What evidence would change that decision?
5. Who owns the next action and by when?

### 8. Follow up and close

Within 14 days, record whether the customer acted, what blocked action, what additional need appeared, whether a referral occurred, and whether the outcome may be used privately for aggregate learning. Close access and delete/retain according to the agreed schedule.

## Offer cards

### Quick Diagnostic — $125

- 15-minute pre-read
- 60-minute facilitated session
- 15-minute written risk/next-step note
- one decision; no custom market model
- target delivery cost: 1.25 hours at the approved labor rate

### Idea Sprint — $750

- decision frame and working session
- focused evidence scan
- customer/offer hypothesis
- small test and first-pass economics
- target delivery: four hours; one revision

### Venture Build — $4,200

- research and evidence register
- operating and go-to-market plan
- 24-month management model appropriate to the concept
- launch brief and decision review
- target delivery: 22 hours; formal scope change after one revision

### Partner Workshop — $2,500

- host planning session
- one facilitated cohort or clinic
- participant worksheet and consent path
- aggregate outcomes report
- no disclosure of participant projects without specific permission

## Case metrics

For every paid case record:

- segment, offer, host/channel, lead date, paid date, price, discount
- scheduled and actual hours by role
- direct labor, materials, processing, and contractor cost
- revisions, rework cause, delay, cancellation, refund
- on-time and completeness status
- customer-reported decision confidence before/after
- action taken by 14 days
- referral, continuation request, or no-action reason
- consent, data closure, correction, and incident status

## Stop and escalation rules

Stop work and escalate when:

- the customer requests regulated or unsafe advice
- identity, authorization, or ownership is materially unclear
- the work appears deceptive, exploitative, illegal, or harmful
- confidential data enters an unapproved system
- a material factual error may affect a decision
- payment, scope, consent, or ownership is disputed
- capacity would push reviewed utilization above 85%

The Founder owns commercial escalation; Trust & Operations owns data/incident escalation; Research & Integrity owns material correction; qualified counsel or specialists own domain advice.

## Weekly pilot review

1. New leads, channel, and qualification
2. Paid conversion and lost reasons
3. Active cases, risk, backlog, and utilization
4. Completed outcomes, hours, cost, rework, and refunds
5. Source and correction issues
6. Consent, accessibility, and incidents
7. Cash, runway, and tranche status
8. One decision to preserve, change, or stop
